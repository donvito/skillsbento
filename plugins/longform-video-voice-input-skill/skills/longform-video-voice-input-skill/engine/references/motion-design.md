# Motion design rules of thumb

## Rhythm
- Pick the BPM first; everything lands on `BEAT = 60/BPM`. Hard cuts land on beats, scene changes on bar lines (4 beats), and the biggest reveal on the downbeat after a riser.
- A 30s reel has about 7–9 scenes: open hook (≤2s), title, 3–4 content scenes, a fast montage, proof (numbers/logos), then CTA. For 15s, cut the content scenes to 2 and drop the montage or proof.
- Vary the tempo: a dense scene, then one that breathes. Alternate dark and light backgrounds (or full-bleed footage and framed footage) so each cut reads.
- Anticipate: a whoosh or riser starts 0.4–0.6s before the cut, and the impact lands on the cut.

## Motion
- Entrances: `outExpo`/`outQuint` (fast in, soft settle) over 0.3–0.6s. Exits and pushes: `inExpo`. Scene-to-scene moves: `ioExpo`.
- Stagger letters by 0.02–0.04s and words by 0.05–0.08s. Never have everything arrive at once.
- One hero motion per moment, with supporting elements 0.1–0.3s later.
- Footage should always move a little: a slow push (`zoom` 1.12→1.0) or parallax. Still images get the Ken Burns treatment.
- Overshoot (`outBack`) suits chips, pills and badges, not headlines.
- Keep shake and flash for real hits. Constant shake reads as amateur.

## Type
- One display face and one mono for labels/HUD. Big and tight headlines (tracking −0.02 to −0.05em); mono labels small, wide-tracked and in caps.
- Hierarchy per frame: one headline, one supporting line, and at most one label.
- Text over footage always gets a scrim. Keep text inside the margins (`M`); portrait needs bigger type relative to width.
- Hold readable text for at least 1s per 3 words (the montage single words are the exception).

## Colour & brand
- Use the brand's own palette and fonts when they exist. A restrained palette (bg, fg, one accent) looks more expensive than many colours.
- Monochrome brands: use gray two-tone for emphasis instead of adding a hue, and a white pill as the CTA.
- Clip colour competes with graphics, so frame loud footage on neutral backgrounds.

## Honesty
- Only real claims: numbers, clients, awards, quotes. Terminal logs, counters and UI mockups are fine as illustration, but tell the user which elements are illustrative.

## Review checklist (on the contact sheet)
- [ ] No overlapping or clipped text; nothing outside the margins (except intentional bleeds)
- [ ] Every transition midpoint looks intentional (not a blank or broken frame)
- [ ] Faces and products are not cropped awkwardly (`focus`)
- [ ] Brand colours and fonts are correct; no leftover template copy or placeholders
- [ ] The CTA is readable and held for at least 2s at the end
